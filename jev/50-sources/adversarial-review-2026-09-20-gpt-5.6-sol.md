## C. Overall verdict

**Not yet fit to serve as ground truth for autonomous product recommendations.** It is unusually rich and often candid about failures, but its decision layer is materially more confident than its evidence layer. The central defect is that it knows calibration and accuracy are task-dependent, yet repeatedly promotes “calibrated confidence,” vendor demos, and nominal latency into generic reasons to recommend deployment.

The five highest-value changes are:

1. Separate vendor claims, API guarantees, demonstrations, and measured task performance; never let a cookbook’s existence alone produce **strong**.
2. Replace every unqualified calibration/abstention claim with target-domain measurement requirements.
3. Repair rollout guidance: preserve authoritative rules, outage fallback, rollback, and monitoring; remove “delete the incumbent.”
4. Add deployment eligibility gates for access, privacy, residency, retention, security, SLA, rate limits, and self-hosting.
5. Fix provenance metadata and retrieval: source model version, cookbook URLs in `index.json`, missing FAQ/count, terminology aliases, and claim-adjacent citations.

Until those changes are made, a sceptical CTO would reasonably call the corpus credulous about calibration and operational readiness, while a TypeSafe engineer could reasonably object that it overgeneralizes both negative language conclusions and the 255-option limit despite TypeSafe’s documented rewrite patterns.
